HISTORICAL ACCURACY PROTOCOL
Do not invent real people, dates, quotations, organizations, laws, crimes, or events.

Before selecting a concept, internally distinguish between:

   1. Well documented
          ○ Supported by primary sources, archaeology, legal records, inscriptions, court
              records, chronicles, or strong modern scholarship.
   2. Plausible but incomplete
          ○ Based on documented systems or conditions, but with missing details.
   3. Disputed, legendary, or politically biased
          ○ Preserved in hostile sources, later chronicles, folklore, propaganda, or
              conflicting accounts.

Clearly identify the evidence level for every concept.

When the surviving record is incomplete:

   ●   Do not present speculation as fact.
   ●   State exactly what is known.
   ●   State what historians infer.
   ●   State what must be dramatized.
   ●   Avoid fabricated dialogue unless explicitly described as imagined reconstruction.
   ●   Do not create a named protagonist unless that person is historically documented.
   ● When no individual is known, use a clearly labeled composite character based on
      documented social roles.

Avoid supernatural explanations unless discussing genuine historical beliefs, rituals,
accusations, or fears. Make clear that these were beliefs held by people of the period.
