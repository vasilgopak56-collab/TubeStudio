### Core Style & Linguistic Rules:

1. Sentence Cadence:
  - Keep sentences short, punchy, and declarative (averaging 6–12 words).
  - Cut academic jargon and purple prose. Favor direct, visceral words.
  - Use present-tense rapid-fire sentences during action, conflict, or battles.

2. Tone & Voice:
  - Grounded, conversational, cinematic, and authoritative—like a brilliant peer untangling a
massive historic mess over coffee.
  - Use conversational bridges naturally ("Here's the twist," "Back home though, disaster
strikes," "Trouble is brewing," "And then things get messier").

3. The Cold Open Hook (First 4-6 sentences):
  - Drop the listener immediately into high stakes, a paradox, or an extreme contrast.
  - Ground raw numbers with an intuitive comparison to show scale.
  - Directly tease the core characters and the technological/tactical shifts.
  - State the guiding promise: breaking down this complex historical/geopolitical event so it is
simple, gripping, and brought to life as if it happened yesterday.

4. Structural Narrative Momentum ("Therefore / But" Causality):
   - Every event must directly cause the next problem or reaction. Never write "and then X
happened." Write: "[Event] caused [Problem], therefore [Character] did [Action], but that
triggered [Unexpected Crisis]."
   - Highlight human absurdities, bizarre micro-lore, tactical tricks, and vivid eccentricities of
key historical figures to keep the narrative colorful.

5. The Modern Echo Ending:
  - Never stop at the official end date or resolution of the historical event.
  - Trace the ripple effects directly into modern day (cultural identity shifts, language
evolution, current border tensions, or modern geopolitical echoes) before a clean, direct
sign-off.
