PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
Write a documentary that keeps viewers watching by combining:

   ●   A dangerous opening situation
   ●   A clear central historical question
   ●   A human point of view
   ●   Escalating danger
   ●   Carefully timed historical revelations
   ●   Reversals and complications
   ●   A strong turning point
   ● A satisfying climax
   ● Meaningful historical consequences
   ● A final insight, contradiction, irony, or unresolved question

The script must deliver the promise established by the title and opening.

Do not artificially withhold the central answer until the final paragraph. Reveal
important information throughout the documentary while allowing each answer to
create a deeper question.

HISTORICAL ACCURACY PROTOCOL
Use the supplied research notes as the primary factual foundation.

Do not invent:

   ●   Historical people
   ●   Dates
   ●   Locations
   ●   Organizations
   ●   Laws
   ●   Political offices
   ●   Crimes
   ●   Battles
   ●   Arrests
   ●   Escapes
   ●   Motives
   ●   Relationships
   ●   Quotations
   ●   Dialogue
   ●   Eyewitness accounts
   ●   Private thoughts
   ●   Exact actions that are not documented

Distinguish internally between:

Documented fact

Information supported by strong historical, legal, archaeological, literary, or scholarly
evidence.

Historical inference

A reasonable conclusion based on known practices, conditions, evidence, or
scholarly interpretation.

Disputed claim
A claim preserved in biased, incomplete, hostile, contradictory, legendary, or much
later sources.

Dramatized reconstruction

A plausible narrative bridge used to illustrate an event whose exact
moment-by-moment details were not recorded.

When evidence is uncertain, use transparent wording such as:

   ●   “The surviving evidence suggests…”
   ●   “Later accounts claimed…”
   ●   “Some historians interpret this as…”
   ●   “The exact sequence is unknown…”
   ●   “One possibility is…”
   ●   “The following sequence is a reconstruction based on…”
   ●   “It is impossible to know whether…”

Do not interrupt the story with constant disclaimers. Place uncertainty naturally at the
moment the questionable claim appears.

Never present invented dialogue or quotations as historical fact.

When no documented individual can carry the story, use a clearly identified
composite viewpoint based on a historically documented profession, social group, or
criminal role. Never give the composite character a name that could be mistaken for a
real historical person.

If the research material is insufficient to support a claim, exclude the claim or place it
in the verification section. Do not fill evidence gaps with confident-sounding fiction.

WORD COUNT AND ACT STRUCTURE
Keep the complete voiceover script within approximately five percent of the requested
word count.

Determine the number of acts using the target word count:

   ●   Up to 1,200 words: 3 acts
   ●   1,201–2,000 words: 4 acts
   ●   2,001–3,200 words: 5 acts
   ●   3,201–4,500 words: 6 acts
   ●   More than 4,500 words: 7 acts

Use this general allocation:

   ● Opening hook and immediate danger: approximately 5–8%
   ● Setup and historical situation: approximately 15–20%
   ● Escalation and investigation: approximately 30–35%
   ● Turning point and complications: approximately 20–25%
   ● Climax: approximately 10–15%
   ● Aftermath and historical meaning: approximately 8–12%

Adapt these percentages when the story demands it, but do not allow background
exposition to dominate the first third.

Each act must perform a distinct narrative function and end with one of the following:

   ●   A consequential discovery
   ●   A reversal
   ●   A new threat
   ●   A difficult choice
   ●   A betrayal
   ●   An unanswered question
   ●   A change in the audience’s understanding
   ●   A consequence that makes retreat impossible

Do not end every act with an exaggerated cliffhanger. The transition should emerge
naturally from the historical story.

NARRATIVE STRUCTURE
Build the documentary around one central historical question.

Examples of suitable narrative questions include:

   ●   How did this criminal network operate?
   ●   Why was this person hunted?
   ●   How did the conspiracy reach the center of power?
   ●   Could the prisoner escape?
   ●   Who benefited from the illegal trade?
   ●   Why did the authorities fail to stop it?
   ●   Was the accused individual truly responsible?
   ●   How much of the surviving account can be trusted?

Do not use these examples unless they fit the selected topic.

The story should generally contain:

Opening

Begin inside a dangerous event, irreversible decision, discovery, betrayal, pursuit,
arrest, infiltration, escape attempt, or moment of imminent consequence.

Setup
Establish the central figure, objective, opposing force, location, and stakes without
pausing for a broad history lesson.

Escalation

Introduce obstacles, hidden systems, conflicting accounts, political pressures,
personal risks, and discoveries that progressively complicate the story.

Turning point

Reveal information or an event that changes the meaning or direction of everything
that came before it.

Climax

Bring the central conflict to its highest point through confrontation, escape, exposure,
arrest, assassination attempt, collapse, or decisive discovery.

Aftermath

Explain the documented consequences and what the story reveals about crime, law,
power, survival, or society in that historical world.

OPENING REQUIREMENTS
The first 100–150 words must:

   ●   Begin during an active and specific moment
   ●   Establish a location or situation
   ●   Introduce immediate danger or irreversible consequences
   ●   Present the central figure or human viewpoint
   ●   Create one clear unanswered question
   ●   Avoid broad civilizational exposition
   ●   Avoid phrases such as “Throughout history”
   ●   Avoid starting with a date followed by a textbook explanation
   ●   Avoid empty cinematic language that contains no information
   ●   Avoid revealing every major answer immediately

The opening should make viewers feel that something has already begun and that
leaving now would mean missing the explanation.

RETENTION REQUIREMENTS
Every paragraph must accomplish at least one of the following:

   ● Reveal new information
   ● Increase danger
   ●    Deepen the mystery
   ●    Introduce an obstacle
   ●    Change the meaning of earlier information
   ●    Clarify the historical system
   ●    Reveal a motive
   ●    Complicate a character
   ●    Narrow the possible outcome
   ●    Deliver a promised answer
   ●    Create a stronger question
   ●    Show a consequence

Remove any paragraph that merely repeats the topic, mood, danger, or central
question.

Use open loops selectively. Every major question introduced must either:

   ● Be answered later
   ● Be explicitly identified as unresolved by the historical record
   ● Lead to a deeper and more important question

Do not manufacture suspense through vague phrases such as:

   ●    “But everything was about to change”
   ●    “What happened next shocked everyone”
   ●    “Little did he know”
   ●    “The truth was darker than anyone imagined”
   ●    “This changed history forever”

Use concrete developments instead.

WRITING STYLE
Write for natural spoken narration.

Use:

   ●    Clear, direct language
   ●    Short and medium-length sentences
   ●    Specific nouns and active verbs
   ●    Concrete physical details supported by the historical setting
   ●    Smooth transitions
   ●    Controlled rhythm
   ●    Occasional longer sentences for explanation
   ●    Concise historical context
   ●    Emotion grounded in documented stakes

Avoid:

   ● Academic prose
   ●    Excessive dates and names
   ●    Repetitive rhetorical questions
   ●    Constant one-sentence paragraphs
   ●    Choppy sentence fragments used only for drama
   ●    Overwritten descriptions
   ●    Melodrama
   ●    Generic suspense language
   ●    Modern slang
   ●    Moralizing
   ●    Excessive metaphors
   ●    Repeated summaries
   ●    Unverified psychological claims
   ●    Telling viewers how to feel
   ●    Statements about what historical characters “must have thought”
   ●    Fake certainty
   ●    Sensational claims unsupported by evidence

Do not use first-person commentary from the writer.

Do not mention AI, animation software, image generation, production tools, or the
content-creation process.
